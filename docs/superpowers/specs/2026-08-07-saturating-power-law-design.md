# Saturating Power Law (`spl`) — Model Registration + User-Set Ceiling

**Date**: 2026-08-07
**Branch**: `time-basis-toggle-phase2b`
**Status**: Design — pending implementation plan

> **Every number in this document is generated by
> `btc_venv/bin/python3 tools/analyze_spl.py`.** The price history grows daily,
> so these are a snapshot taken at n=5801 (t = 0.977 … 16.857 yr). Re-run the

## 1. Goal

Register a new price model, **`spl` — "Saturating Power Law"**:

```
price   = L / (1 + (t/t₀)^(−β))
log10 p = log10(L) − log10(1 + (t/t₀)^(−β))
```

A power law that rolls over to a finite ceiling. Early (`t ≪ t₀`) it is
`price ≈ L·(t/t₀)^β` — the familiar Bitcoin power law. Late (`t ≫ t₀`) it
approaches `L`. At `t = t₀`, price is exactly `L/2`.

`t` is years since the 2009-07-25 origin, matching every other model so the
fit is comparable.

**It is a logistic in log-time.** Substituting `(t/t₀)^(−β) = exp(−β(ln t − ln t₀))`:

```
price = L / (1 + exp(−β·(ln t − ln t₀)))
```

which is the existing `logi` model with `ln t` in place of `t`. On a log-log
chart, where the power law is a straight line, `spl` is an S-curve — a power
law that bends at both ends.

## 3. What the data actually say

This section is the reason the model is worth having, and it must survive
into the Model Info card rather than living only here.

### 3.1 The fit

| | β / slope | t₀ | L (per BTC) | market cap | RMSE (log10) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| power law | 5.0734 | — | — | — | 0.294318 |
| **`spl`** | **5.0905** | **28.371 yr** | **$1,649,528** | **$34.6T** | **0.294261** |
| exponential | — | — | — | — | 0.558625 |

`ΔAIC = −0.25` (a dead heat, hair in favour of `spl`); **`ΔBIC = +6.42`,
so `pl` is preferred.** With n=5801, BIC punishes the third parameter harder
than it earns. The honest verdict is that the extra parameter does not pay
for itself.

### 3.2 The ceiling is bounded below, not above

Profiling SSE with `t₀` held fixed:

| t₀ (yr) | RMSE | L (per BTC) | market cap | ΔSSE vs best |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 0.295245 | $315,790 | $7T | +3.36420 |
| 25 | 0.294302 | $891,424 | $19T | +0.13918 |
| **28.4** | **0.294261** | **$1,657,890** | **$35T** | **0.00000** |
| 35 | 0.294285 | $4,684,498 | $98T | +0.08157 |
| 50 | 0.294312 | $28,300,751 | $594T | +0.17272 |
| 100 | 0.294318 | $950,544,667 | $19,961T | +0.19416 |
| 1000 | 0.294318 | $1.1×10¹⁴ | — | +0.19484 |

The surface is **steep below t₀≈25 and flat above ≈50**, asymptoting to the
`pl` value. Within 1 AIC unit: `t₀ ∈ {25, 28.4, 35, 50}` yr — i.e. **L
anywhere from $19T to $594T, a 30× range.** `pl` itself falls *outside* that
band.

So the identified quantity is a **floor under the ceiling**: conditional on
this functional form, Bitcoin cannot top out below roughly $19T total cap.
The data cannot distinguish $35T from $594T from no ceiling at all. Any
single displayed L is a point on a nearly flat ridge, and the card must say
so.

### 3.3 What the model claims about the future

`spl − pl` reduces exactly to `−log10(1 + (t/t₀)^β)` (verified numerically to
1.8×10⁻¹⁵):

| year | `spl` vs power law |
|---|---|
| 2020 | −0.61% |
| 2024 | −3.11% |
| 2026 | −5.85% |
| 2030 | −15.84% |
| 2038 (= t₀) | −50.29% |
| 2050 | −85.86% |

**The model's entire evidence base is a ~6% shortfall against the power law
today**, and from it the model extrapolates a halving by 2038. Residual
scatter around the power law is 0.294 log10 — a factor of two — so that
signal is roughly a tenth of the noise, visible only because there are 5,801
daily points.

A caveat that cuts against the model: measured log-log curvature is very
slightly **convex** (quadratic coefficient +0.029), the opposite sign from
saturation. `spl` is not fitting a global bend; it is fitting localised
recent under-performance at the right edge and extrapolating it.

### 3.4 Linear-time saturation is decisively rejected

The counterpart `price = L / (1 + exp(−r(t − t₀)))` — saturation on a linear
rather than logarithmic timescale:

| | L | t₀ | r | RMSE | ΔBIC vs pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| linear-time | **$124,642 — pinned to the highest observed price** | 12.55 yr | 0.9506 | 0.450283 | **+4942** |

It drives the ceiling down to exactly the highest price ever seen — it wants
to declare Bitcoin already saturated — and pays 4942 BIC for it. This
independently reproduces the pathology CLAUDE.md documents for `logi`
("saturates below current BTC price"). **Saturation is a log-time
phenomenon or it is nothing.**

This result gets a figure in the Model Info card (§7.3).

## 4. Fitting

`tools/fit_spl.py`, differential evolution over log10 residuals, `--update`
patching the class attrs — the pattern of the existing `fit_*.py` tools.

**Internal parameterisation is `(A, β, log10 t₀)`** where
`A = log10 L − β·log10 t₀` is the power-law intercept. `A` and `β` are well
determined; this collapses the near-degenerate direction onto the single
coordinate `log10 t₀` instead of smearing it across all three. `log10 L` is
recovered as `A + β·log10 t₀` and stored on the class.

**Bounds**

| param | bound | rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `L` | ≤ **$1,000T total cap** ($47.6M/BTC) | Clears the entire 1-AIC band, so the bound never binds inside the statistically plausible range. |
| `L` | ≥ highest observed price | A ceiling below the data is meaningless. |
| `t₀` | [1, 100] yr | Fitted value 28.4 is comfortably interior. |
| `β` | (0.01, 20] | Observed ≈5.09. |

**$100T is a display/sanity annotation, not a bound.** It is reached at
**t₀ ≈ 35.1 yr** — inside the 1-AIC band — so making it a hard cap would
produce a reported ceiling that is a property of our bound rather than the
data. The Model Info card notes when the fit exceeds it.

**The fit report must state whether any bound is active.** A pinned parameter
means the reported value is an artifact; that has to be visible, not inferred.

### 4.1 Pipeline integration — checklist Phase D, all three ⚠️ steps

These are flagged in `workflow_new_model.md` as the gaps that bit v1, and
each one fails **silently**: the model still renders, so nothing looks wrong.
`spl`'s closest sibling `logi` has all three, and they are the template.

| Step | File | Change | What breaks if skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | `tools/build_bm_model.py` (`_build_model_instances`, beside `instances["logi"]` at `:96`) | `instances["spl"] = bc.SaturatingPowerLawModel(price_years, price_prices, quantiles)` | **This is the price-update integration.** `update_prices.py:142` runs `build_bm_model.py` after appending new rows, and `instances` feeds `resqr_coefs` and `diagnostics` into `model_data.pkl`. Without it `spl` is absent from every rebuild — no resqr bundle, no diagnostics — and the app still renders it from the hardcoded class attrs, so nothing visibly fails. |
| 20 | `tools/refit_all_ppl.py` (beside `("Logistic", "tools/fit_logistic.py")` at `:59`) | append `("SatPL", "tools/fit_spl.py")` | `spl` is never refit by the sweep; its parameters freeze at whatever was last hardcoded, drifting further from the data with every price update. |
| 21 | `tools/fit_shrinking_sigma.py` | add an `elif model_name == "spl":` branch computing residuals from the model's log10 formula (pattern: the `logi` branch at `:219`), and append `"spl"` to `models = [...]` at `:248` | No offline shrinking-σ parameters. `_init_shrinking_bands` still derives σ at construction, so bands render — but `spl` is silently missing from the 12-model sweep its siblings all participate in. |

The common failure mode is that **all three are invisible at runtime.** The
model appears on the chart either way, which is precisely why v1 missed them.
The plan must verify each with an assertion, not by looking at the chart.

**Refit policy: refit all three freely**, as every sibling model does.
Justification: there is no scheduled refit timer installed (`refit_all_ppl.py`
is run manually), the $1,000T bound will not bind, and Phase 2 lets the user
override `L` regardless. The card quotes the 1-AIC band so a shifted point
estimate reads as expected rather than alarming.

## 5. Model class

`btc_core/_simple.py`, beside its siblings:

```python
class SaturatingPowerLawModel(_ShrinkingBandsMixin):
    name        = "Saturating Power Law"
    short_name  = "spl"           # PERMANENT — baked into every share link
    legend_name = "SatPL"
    quantized   = True

    _log10_L = ...   # patched by tools/fit_spl.py --update
    _t0      = ...
    _beta    = ...

    def _model_log10(self, t):
        t_arr = np.asarray(t, float)
        u = -self._beta * (np.log(t_arr) - np.log(self._t0))
        return self._log10_L - np.logaddexp(0.0, u) / np.log(10.0)
```

**`logaddexp` is defensive, not load-bearing at current bounds.** An earlier
draft of this spec claimed the naive `(t/t₀)**(-beta)` form *overflows*; the
build gate refuted that. Measured largest intermediate at the data's left edge
(t = 0.977):

| β | `(t/t₀)^(−β)` |
|---|---|
| 5.09 (fitted) | 2.8×10⁷ |
| 20 (fitting bound) | 1.8×10²⁹ |
| 90 | 4.7×10¹³¹ |
| 210 | 1.7×10³⁰⁷ — at the float64 ceiling |

So within `β ≤ 20` the naive form is comfortably finite, and both forms agree.
`logaddexp` is used because it is the idiomatic stable spelling and costs
nothing, and because it keeps headroom if the β bound is ever widened —
overflow begins near β≈210 here, and near β≈90 at t=0.1. It does **not**
rescue a computation that is otherwise broken today. Keep it, but do not
justify it with a claim the numbers don't support.

Bands via `_init_shrinking_bands(t, residuals, quantiles)`, identical to
`gomp` / `logi` / `sexp` / `bpl`.

## 8. Phase 2 — user-parameterised ceiling

**Placement.** A section card in the Tab 1 controls column, revealed when
`spl` is ticked in Display Models. This is exactly the U₁ mechanism
(`callbacks/user_model.py:127-135`): a clientside callback on
`bub-model-show` toggling the panel's `style`. No activation checkbox, no
modal.

**Not a gear modal.** The existing config modals (`lppl-config-modal`,
`bm-config-modal`, …) are full-screen and cover the chart. The entire point
here is dragging the ceiling while watching the fit degrade; a modal makes
that impossible.

**Control.** A log-scale slider labelled in **total market cap**, matching
how the bounds were reasoned about, with `L = cap / 21e6`. Marks at $1T /
$10T / $35T / $100T / $1000T. Log scale because the range spans ~2.5 decades
— the same treatment `bub-yrange` already uses in that column.

**`updatemode="mouseup"`.** One drag is one refit, not eighty. A
Promise-return debounce was tried on these sliders before and broke them
live; `mouseup` is the recorded alternative.

**Refit.** With `L` fixed, only `(t₀, β)` are free — a 2-parameter fit,
sub-100 ms server-side.

**Live fit-quality readout** beside the slider: RMSE and ΔBIC vs `pl`. This
is the payoff. At $2T the readout turns to roughly `RMSE 0.51, ΔBIC +5900`
and the curve visibly peels off the data — the user *sees* why a low ceiling
is untenable, with a number attached.

Plus **Reset to best fit**.

**Session-only** — a memory `dcc.Store`, like U₁. Not in `_SNAPSHOT_CONTROLS`
(deferred to post-Phase-2).
