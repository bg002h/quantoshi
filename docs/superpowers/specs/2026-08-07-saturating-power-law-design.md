## 1. Goal

Register **`spl` — "Saturating Power Law"**:

```
price   = L / (1 + (t/t₀)^(−β))
log10 p = log10(L) − log10(1 + (t/t₀)^(−β))
```

Early (`t ≪ t₀`), `price ≈ L·(t/t₀)^β` — the Bitcoin power law. Late, it
approaches `L`. At `t = t₀`, price is `L/2`. `t` is years since 2009-07-25.

It is a **logistic in log-time**: substituting `(t/t₀)^(−β) = exp(−β(ln t − ln t₀))`
gives the existing `logi` model with `ln t` for `t`. On a log-log chart, where
the power law is a straight line, `spl` is an S-curve.

### 3.1 The finding: L is not stable under more data

The ceiling is not a quantity this dataset measures. Two windows, same code,
same test — the second a strict superset adding 1.1% more rows:

| | data through 2026-06-03 | data through 2026-08-06 |
|---|---|---|
| n | 5,792 | 5,856 |
| last price | $64,813 | $64,294 |
| **L (market cap)** | **$34.3T** | **$14.8T** |
| t₀ (yr) | 28.31 | 23.87 |
| β | 5.0910 | 5.1040 |
| RMSE | 0.294470 | 0.293851 |
| variance removed vs `pl` | 0.0394% | 0.2329% |
| LRT statistic | 2.2802 | 13.6536 |
| vs boundary crit 2.7055 | fail to reject | **REJECTS** |
| p | 0.066 | 0.0001 |

**The prices differ by less than 1% and the ceiling estimate differs by 2.3×.**
So L does not track price *level* — it tracks the recent trajectory relative to
trend, which is a stronger statement than the raw range, because it forecloses
the obvious objection that a lower price simply implies a lower ceiling.

Across six cutoffs the instability is systematic, not a one-off:

| cutoff | last price | L ($T) | t₀ | LRT | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024-08-06 | $56,308 | 10.7 | 22.4 | 6.01 | REJECTS |
| 2025-02-06 | $97,846 | 44.1 | 29.8 | 0.51 | fail to reject |
| 2025-08-06 | $114,663 | **1,000** ⚠️cap | 55.1 | −0.11 | fail to reject |
| 2026-02-06 | $67,196 | **1,000** ⚠️cap | 55.0 | −0.14 | fail to reject |
| 2026-06-03 | $64,813 | 34.3 | 28.3 | 2.28 | fail to reject |
| 2026-08-06 | $64,294 | 14.8 | 23.9 | 13.65 | REJECTS |

L ranges over **93×**, and the verdict flips **non-monotonically** — rejects,
then four windows of not-rejecting, then rejects again. A parameter behaving
this way is not being estimated.

**Reading the two capped rows.** `L` pinned at the $1000T fitting bound is not
a fitting failure: `L → ∞` **is** the pure power law, so those windows are the
data asking for *no ceiling at all*. That also explains the negative LRT there
— `spl` nests `pl`, so the statistic cannot be negative at a true optimum, and
−0.11 / −0.14 are the residue of the bound blocking convergence to the `pl`
limit. Reported rather than clipped to zero, because the sign is informative.

**What supersedes what.** Earlier revisions led with "even assuming iid we fail
to reject t₀ = ∞" (LRT 2.2802 vs 2.7055). That statement was true of the
2026-06-03 window and is **false today**. It is retracted as a headline. The
conclusion it supported — L unidentified — is unchanged and now rests on the
instability above, which no future price move can embarrass, because the
instability *is* the result.

### 3.2 Why iid is the generous case

Residual autocorrelation, `pl` vs `spl`, against what an AR(1) with the same
lag-1 ρ would predict:

| lag | `pl` | `spl` | AR(1) predicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 d | +0.9981 | +0.9981 | +0.9981 |
| 30 d | +0.9032 | +0.9032 | +0.9453 |
| 180 d | +0.3546 | +0.3557 | +0.7136 |
| **365 d** | **−0.2228** | −0.2234 | +0.5044 |
| **730 d** | **−0.4011** | −0.4025 | +0.2544 |
| 1095 d | +0.1446 | +0.1469 | +0.1283 |

Two things follow.

**`pl` and `spl` have the same residuals.** Lag-1 ρ differs by −7×10⁻⁷. The
saturating term removes 0.039% of the variance and essentially none of the
autocorrelation — it does not engage the structure that is actually in this
series.

**The error process is oscillatory, so no AR(1) fits it at any ρ.** An AR(1)
ACF is strictly positive and monotone-decaying; this one crosses zero at 287
days and is negative through ~2 years. That is the halving cycle in the
autocorrelation function. Consequently every parametric autocorrelation
correction was discarded (§3.5), and for a low-frequency contrast like trend
curvature, correlated errors only ever *reduce* information relative to iid.

### 3.3 The ceiling is bounded below, not above

> **Data window:** §§3.3–3.6 are computed on the **2026-06-03** snapshot
> (n=5,792), the window in hand when they were written. §3.1 supersedes them
> wherever they conflict, and its whole point is that these point estimates
> move. They are retained because the *shapes* they show — a flat profile, a
> cycle that dominates the curvature — are what the argument rests on, and
> those are stable even as the numbers are not. Re-run
> `tools/analyze_spl.py` for current values.

Profiling SSE with t₀ held fixed (2026-06-03 window):

| t₀ (yr) | RMSE | market cap | ΔSSE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 0.295445 | $7T | +3.33134 |
| 25 | 0.294509 | $19T | +0.13524 |
| **28.4** | **0.294470** | **$35T** | **0.00000** |
| 35 | 0.294494 | $98T | +0.08364 |
| 50 | 0.294521 | $595T | +0.17547 |
| 1000 | 0.294528 | — | +0.19771 |

Steep below, flat above, asymptoting to `pl`. This is the well-known
**pre-inflection sigmoid problem** (carrying capacity in ecology, early-epidemic
ceiling fits): *the upper asymptote is unidentifiable until the inflection is
inside the sample.* Log-log Bitcoin has not visibly inflected.

So L is **one-sided estimable**: a floor near the current trend level, and no
upper bound whatsoever. The fitted t₀ = 28.3 yr sits **1.7× beyond the last
observation**.

The by-cutoff refits show what the estimator is really doing — t₀ comes back
finite exactly when the window ends *below* trend:

| data through | t₀ | final-year residual |
|---|---|---|
| 2018 | 6.2 yr | −0.10 (below) |
| 2019 | 5,361 yr | +0.34 (above) |
| 2022 | 6,978 yr | +0.20 (above) |
| **2024** | **29.7 yr** | −0.21 (below) |
| 2026 | 7,039 yr | +0.06 (above) |

**t₀ is a cycle-phase detector, not a ceiling detector.**

### 3.5 Sensitivity: does modelling the 4-year cycle change the answer?

The obvious objection is that the cycle masks the curvature. It is worth
taking seriously, because **it half-works** — and the honest account of why it
does not rescue the ceiling is the most informative thing in this section.

Adding a calendar sinusoid `C·cos(2πt/P + φ)` to the mean:

*(2026-06-03 window — see the §3.3 note.)*

| model | RMSE | t₀ | cycle P |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pl` | 0.294528 | — | — |
| `spl` | 0.294470 | 28.3 yr | — |
| `pl` + cycle | 0.220787 | — | 3.56 yr |
| **`spl` + cycle** | **0.217910** | **19.0 yr** | 3.58 yr |

The cycle removes **43.8%** of variance — against `spl`'s 0.039%. *The cycle
is the real signal in this series; the saturation term is rounding error beside
it.* And conditioning on it raises the iid test statistic from 2.28 to
**151.894**, which would reject `t₀ = ∞` outright. The cycle and the curvature
are not orthogonal over a finite window containing 4.4 non-integer cycles, so
the unmodelled cycle was partially masking the bend.

Three checks establish that the conditional result is nonetheless an artifact:

1. **The null is not under-converged.** Seeding `pl+cycle` from the
   alternative's cycle parameters gives a *worse* fit (SSE 466 vs 282), so the
   151.894 is real arithmetic, not an optimiser failure.
2. **90.9% of the improvement accrues in the final two years** (6.64 of 7.31),
   and the cumulative path changes sign twice along the way — `spl` was
   *losing* through 2018–2020. This is a terminal excursion, not a systematic
   bend.
3. **Conditional t₀ sat at its upper bound (100 yr) for every cutoff from 2020
   through 2024**, becoming finite only in 2025 (33.0) and 2026 (18.0–19.0),
   and still moving fast. That is the opposite of convergence.

And the statistic itself does not survive the dependence it ignores. The cycle
model barely whitens anything — lag-1 ρ goes 0.9981 → 0.9966, DW 0.0037 →
0.0068. With 4.4 cycles of data, 151.894 scales to **0.116** against a critical
value of 2.706.

The framing that makes the exact effective sample size irrelevant:
**significance would require n_eff ≥ 103 — residual decorrelation within ~56
days — but the measured 30-day residual ACF is 0.90.** Even a generous
n_eff = 16 (one per year) gives 0.42. The conclusion is insensitive to an order
of magnitude of n_eff choices.

Stated honestly: conditioning on the cycle raised the *real* evidence roughly
50-fold. It remains 10–25× short of the bar.

Note what t₀ = 19 would *claim*: price reaching L/2 within ~2 years, a ceiling
about 2× trend — binding now. Check 3 shows that claim was absent at every
cutoff through 2024 and appeared only with the current drawdown.

### 3.6 Methods discarded, and why

Recorded so they are not re-attempted.

| method | why discarded |
|---|---|
| Scalar `n_eff = n(1−ρ)/(1+ρ) ≈ 5.4` | Derived for the *mean* of a stationary series; too pessimistic for slopes generally. Effective sample size is frequency-dependent — but for *this* lowest-frequency contrast it is of order the cycle count (~4), so the magnitude was accidentally right. |
| AR(1)-GLS (Cochrane–Orcutt) | Refuted by §3.2 — the ACF changes sign. Also unstable: CO and Prais–Winsten differ by 0.32 in the `pl` exponent purely over whether observation 1 is retained, a near-unit-root artifact. |
| Moving-block **pairs** bootstrap | `t` is a fixed exhaustive grid, not a random sample, so resampling (t, price) rows randomises the design, not the error process. A **residual** block bootstrap would be correct if an interval is ever wanted. |
| Walk-forward out-of-sample | Retained as colour only, not evidence. `spl` nests `pl`, so naive OOS comparison is biased against the larger model (Clark–West applies); with overlapping windows the honest number of comparisons is 2–4, not 12–17. |
| ADF on the residuals | Reported as −2.666 against the *standard* 5% critical value of −2.86. Residuals from a fitted trend require Engle–Granger critical values (~−3.4). Same direction, previously miscited. |

## 4. Fitting

`tools/fit_spl.py`, differential evolution on log10 residuals, `--update`
patching class attrs — the pattern of the existing `fit_*.py` tools.

**Data mask `t >= T_MIN` (1.0)**, matching the constructor and
`fit_shrinking_sigma.py`'s branch. All three must agree; fitting on `t > 0`
optimises a different residual set than `_init_shrinking_bands` derives σ from.

**Parameterisation `(A, β, log10 t₀)`**, `A = log10 L − β·log10 t₀`. `A` and
`β` are well determined; this collapses the degenerate direction onto
`log10 t₀` alone.

| param | bound | mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| `t₀` | [1, 100] yr | box bound on `log10 t₀` |
| `β` | (0.01, 20] | box bound |
| `L` | [max observed price, $1000T cap] | **constraint on a derived quantity** — `L = 10^(A + β·log10 t₀)` is not a coordinate, so DE cannot box-bound it. Implemented as a penalty returning `1e6 + 1e3·(distance outside)` from the objective. |

Verified: under exactly these bounds the fit is **interior on all three**
(β=5.1040, t₀=23.87, L=$14.8T on data through 2026-08-06). **The $1000T cap
does bind on some windows** — the 2025-08 and 2026-02 cutoffs both pin L
there (§3.1). A pin is not a ceiling estimate: `L → ∞` is the pure power law,
so a pinned fit is the data asking for no ceiling at all, and `fit_spl.py`
reports it as an active bound rather than a measurement. Earlier revisions
claimed the cap never binds; that held only for the window then in hand.

**$100T is a display annotation, not a bound** — reached at t₀ ≈ 35.1 yr.

**The fit report must state which bounds are active**, including the derived-`L`
penalty. A pinned parameter is an artifact and must be visible.

**Refit policy: refit all three freely.** No scheduled refit timer exists
(`refit_all_ppl.py` is manual), a refit may land on the cap, and Phase 2 lets the
user override `L` anyway.

## 5. Model class

`btc_core/_simple.py`, beside its siblings:

```python
class SaturatingPowerLawModel(_ShrinkingBandsMixin):
    name        = "Saturating Power Law"
    short_name  = "spl"           # PERMANENT — in every share link
    legend_name = "SatPL"
    dash_style  = "longdash"      # REQUIRED — mixin supplies none
    quantized   = True

    _log10_L = ...   # patched by tools/fit_spl.py --update
    _t0      = ...
    _beta    = ...

    def __init__(self, price_years, price_prices, quantiles,
                 log10_L=None, t0=None, beta=None):
        # Optional overrides shadow the class attrs. Phase 2 needs a
        # per-request instance with a user-set ceiling; deciding the
        # signature now avoids changing it after the model ships.
        if log10_L is not None: self._log10_L = log10_L
        if t0      is not None: self._t0 = t0
        if beta    is not None: self._beta = beta
        mask = price_years >= T_MIN
        t = price_years[mask]
        residuals = np.log10(price_prices[mask]) - self._model_log10(t)
        self._init_shrinking_bands(t, residuals, quantiles)
        self._build_colors()

    def _model_log10(self, t):
        t_arr = np.asarray(t, float)
        u = -self._beta * (np.log(t_arr) - np.log(self._t0))
        return self._log10_L - np.logaddexp(0.0, u) / np.log(10.0)

    def _build_colors(self):
        """Violet gradient — REQUIRED, the mixin does not define it."""
        self.colors = {}
        n = len(self.quantiles)
        for i, q in enumerate(self.quantiles):
            frac = i / max(n - 1, 1)
            r = int(76 + 60 * frac); g = int(29 + 40 * frac); b = int(149 + 40 * frac)
            self.colors[q] = f"#{r:02x}{g:02x}{b:02x}"
```

`dash_style`, `__init__` (with the `T_MIN` mask) and `_build_colors` were all
missing from the previous revision; each is a **loud** failure —
`AttributeError`, plus `test_models.py::test_all_models_have_dash_style`.

**`logaddexp` is defensive, not load-bearing.** Measured largest intermediate
at the data's left edge: 2.8×10⁷ at the fitted β, 1.8×10²⁹ at the β≤20 bound —
both far inside float64. Overflow starts near β≈210. Keep it as the idiomatic
stable spelling; do not justify it with an overflow claim the numbers refute.

## 7. Model Info card

The card is where §3 reaches users, and it is the reason the statistics were
worth doing. It must be a **discussion of the findings**, not a formula and a
parameter dump. `spl` is a diagnostic model: its value to a reader is what it
demonstrates about the limits of the data, not its forecast.

### 7.1 Required content, in order

1. **The formula**, and that it is a logistic in log-time — a power law that
   bends at both ends. Note it reduces exactly to `pl` as t₀→∞.

2. **The headline finding: the ceiling moves when you add data.** Lead with
   the side-by-side from §3.1, because it is self-evidencing — a reader does
   not have to trust a statistic to read two columns:

   > Refitting this model on data through 3 June puts the ceiling at $34
   > trillion. Refitting it nine weeks later, with prices within 1% of where
   > they were, puts it at $15 trillion. Across the last two years the same
   > fit has returned anything from $11 trillion to "no ceiling at all."
   > That is not a measurement narrowing in on a value; it is a number that
   > follows the recent price.

   Then the limit it implies:

   > So we don't claim Bitcoin has no ceiling. We claim that seventeen years
   > of data can put a floor under one but cannot put a number on it.

   **Show the ⚠️cap rows.** Two of the six windows pin at the fitting bound,
   and the plain-language reading is the most striking line available: on
   that data the model asks for *no ceiling at all*.

3. **Why**, in one paragraph a non-statistician can follow: this is the
   pre-inflection sigmoid problem. The upper asymptote of an S-curve is
   unidentifiable until the bend is inside the data. Reference the profile
   table (§3.3): a low ceiling fits visibly worse, everything above ~$100T
   fits equally well, and "no ceiling at all" fits essentially as well as the
   best fit.

4. **The fitted values, with the identification caveat attached** — never a
   bare point estimate. β ≈ 5.09, t₀ ≈ 28 yr, L ≈ $34T, and immediately: the
   inflection is **1.7× beyond the last observation**, and refitting with data
   through different years gives t₀ anywhere from 6 to 7,000 years.

5. **The cycle-phase table (§3.3).** This is the most persuasive item on the
   card and needs no statistics to read: t₀ comes back finite exactly when the
   data window happens to end below trend. Label it plainly — *the fitted
   ceiling tracks where we are in the four-year cycle, not where the ceiling
   is.*

6. **The cycle sensitivity (§3.5), honestly.** Note first that on the current
   window the *unconditional* test already rejects (LRT 13.65) — so the card
   must not say "we fail to reject". The honest framing is that significance
   here is a symptom of the instability, not evidence against it: the same
   test failed to reject on four of the last six windows. Then give the cycle
   result — modelling the four-year cycle also makes the saturation term look
   significant — and why neither is believed: 91% of the gain is the last two
   years, the conditional t₀ sat at its bound until 2025, and significance
   would require the residuals to decorrelate within ~56 days when the
   measured 30-day autocorrelation is 0.90. A reader who has thought about
   this will raise the cycle objection; the card should get there first.

7. **What the cycle result says about the app.** The cycle removes 43.8% of
   variance against `spl`'s 0.039%. Point readers at the LPPL/HybPPL/EPPL
   family, which models that structure explicitly. This is the one place where
   `spl` teaches something actionable: the dominant feature of the residuals
   is cyclical, not curvature.

8. **The linear-time rejection (§3.6)** with the comparison figure — saturation
   on a linear rather than logarithmic timescale fits far worse.

9. **Suggested wording for the cycle clause**, if a short version is needed:

   > Even models that account for Bitcoin's four-year cycle cannot pin the
   > ceiling down — the apparent bend they find sits right at the edge of the
   > data, where it cannot be distinguished from the current cycle's swing.

### 7.1a The card is PINNED to two dates (user decision, 2026-08-07)

Every figure on this card is a **static constant fixed to its stated data
window**. Nothing recomputes at render time — not the prose, not the
comparison tables, and **not the coefficient table**, which earlier drafts of
this spec required to be live.

The reason is structural, not stylistic. The card's whole argument is a
comparison of two specific snapshots. If any part of it recomputed, the
"2026-08-06" column would silently become "whenever the page was loaded", the
two dates would stop being two dates, and the comparison carrying the thesis
would dissolve. **A card arguing that this number moves must itself hold
still.**

Requirements:
- Static constants, not `_app_ctx.PRICE_MODELS["spl"]` lookups.
- Every table carries its data-window label. All four, not three.
- One visible line near the top stating the card is a fixed snapshot **and**
  that the plotted curve always uses the latest fit — because
  `fit_spl.py --update` moves the chart while the card stays put, and a reader
  must not discover that as an apparent inconsistency.
- A comment at the constants naming the regeneration path:
  `tools/analyze_spl.py` sections [0] and [0b], with the date labels updated in
  the same edit.

Refreshing the card is a deliberate manual act, scheduled whenever the numbers
are worth restating — not a side effect of the daily price update.

### 7.2 Tone and prohibitions

- **Never quote a single fitted ceiling as "the" ceiling.** Every dollar
  figure on this card must appear beside at least one other window's value, or
  beside the 93× range. A lone point estimate is the one thing this model
  cannot support.
- **Do not say "we fail to reject".** True on the 2026-06-03 window, false on
  the current one. Say the verdict is unstable across windows, which is true
  on all of them.
- **"Consistent with", never "excludes".** The data exclude nothing on the
  high side.
- **Never present a ceiling as a forecast.** Every dollar figure on this card
  is conditional on a shape the data cannot confirm.
- **RMSE to 4 decimals.** Six implies discrimination that is not there.
- Do not repeat the ΔAIC/ΔBIC numbers from earlier revisions; the
  boundary-corrected LRT (§3.1) supersedes them, and the earlier framing
  ("`pl` preferred") was wrong. The correct frame is **L unidentified; `pl` is
  the parsimonious representative of an equivalence class the data cannot
  distinguish.** Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

### 7.3 Comparison figure

Pre-rendered PNG at `btc_web/assets/spl_linear_vs_log.png`, generated by a
dev-only tool in the `render_chart_previews.py` mould.

**Never generated at request time** — `kaleido`/`matplotlib` are not in
`btc_web/requirements.txt`, and importing them on the runtime path passes every
local test and then breaks prod startup.

A second figure is worth considering and is cheap: the **profile curve** —
SSE against log₁₀L, flat from the optimum out to the bound. One picture makes
the identification problem obvious in a way no table does. Optional for
Phase 1.

## 8. Phase 2 — user-set ceiling

**Placement.** Section card in the Tab 1 controls column, revealed when `spl`
is ticked in Display Models — the U₁ mechanism
(`callbacks/user_model.py:127-135`), a clientside callback on `bub-model-show`
toggling `style`. **Verified in R0 that `_DEPRIORITIZED` affects ordering only**
(`layout/display_models.py:212-214`), so `spl` remains a normal checkable
value. Cost: it sits at the bottom of a ~20-row checklist, so the panel is
behind a scroll — a discoverability tradeoff, not a defect.

Not a gear modal: those are full-screen and cover the chart, and the point is
watching the fit degrade while dragging.

**Control.** Log-scale slider in **total market cap** (`L = cap / 21e6`),
marks at $1T / $10T / $35T / $100T / $1000T. Log because the range spans ~2.5
decades, as `bub-yrange` already does in that column.

**Two things Phase 2 must do that are easy to get wrong:**

- **A per-request instance, never a mutated singleton.** `_resolve_model`
  (`figures/common.py:1255-1268`) returns `_app_ctx.PRICE_MODELS.get(key)` —
  a module-level singleton shared by every request in every gunicorn worker.
  Setting `PRICE_MODELS["spl"]._log10_L` from a callback is a **cross-user
  data race**. `u1` is the only key with a per-request escape hatch
  (`UserModel.from_store_dict`, `:1263-1267`); add the same branch for `spl`.
  §5's optional constructor args exist for this.
- **The ceiling must be in the params dict AND in `tab_defaults`.** The params
  dict is the cache key (`cache.py:35`, `sha256(params_json)`): omit it and
  two users with different ceilings share one cached figure; include it but
  omit it from `bubble_defaults()` and the prewarm key stops matching the
  runtime key, costing Tab 1 its first-visit L1 hit. `user_model` does exactly
  this (`tab_defaults.py:506/540/553/564/573`) — mirror it.

**Refit cost.** Constructing a `_ShrinkingBandsMixin` model over the full
history is **1.2–1.8 ms** warm (measured in R0); a 2-parameter local refit
seeded at the optimum is a few ms more. No background-callback machinery
needed. But frame the budget as **one full bubble redraw per mouseup** — the
dominant cost is `build_bubble_figure` plus dispatching a large figure, not
the fit.

`updatemode` is left at its default; `dcc.Slider` already commits on release
and no layout module sets it. It is not a mitigation, just a thing not to
break.

**Live fit-quality readout** (RMSE, ΔBIC vs `pl`) and **Reset to best fit**.
The readout assumes independent residuals, which §3.2 refutes, so it will
look far more confident than the data warrant — label it "at face value"
rather than presenting it as a verdict. Qualitatively it still shows the right thing: drag the ceiling low and
the fit visibly collapses.

**Session-only** — a memory `dcc.Store`, like U₁. Not in `_SNAPSHOT_CONTROLS`.
