Power Law (2 params, 6mo windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t)

Power Law (PL) — the simplest Bitcoin price model. Pure log-log linear fit: price grows as a fixed power of time since genesis. Two parameters (intercept A, slope B) and no oscillation. Sliding-window fits reveal how the effective growth exponent evolves as different eras of Bitcoin history enter and leave the fit window. Here we fit by unbounded OLS (closed-form) so the true slope is never clipped; a centered intercept A' = mean log-price in the window is also plotted — A' is orthogonal to B, while raw A is a mechanical mirror of B (see FAQ on A/B anti-correlation).

Fastest-reacting PL window — 6 months of data. B is essentially a noisy 6-month trailing growth rate; raw panel looks jittery but leads 1yr and 2yr signals by half a window. Useful for very-early warning of regime shifts. See the corresponding timing section at the bottom of /E for the lead/lag structure.

LPPL₁ (6 params, 5yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t) + C·t⁻ᴰ·cos(ω·ln t + φ)

LPPL₁ (Log-Periodic Power Law, 1 frequency) — Quantoshi's damped variant of Sornette's classic bubble model: power-law trend plus one log-periodic oscillation whose peaks compress in log-time while amplitude decays as t⁻ᴰ. No finite-time singularity (unlike classic LPPL). 6 parameters. The angular frequency ω encodes the bubble's discrete scale invariance; historically ω≈7 was the canonical value, but modern Bitcoin fits prefer ω≈15–20 (cycle-stretching). D is the critical exponent.

Single damped log-periodic oscillation. W hits upper bound (15) from ~2020 onward — a signature of Bitcoin's cycle stretching.

LPPL₂ (9 params, 5yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t) + C₁·t⁻ᴰ·cos(ω₁·ln t + φ₁) + C₂·cos(ω₂·ln t + φ₂)

LPPL₂ (2 frequencies) — adds a second, undamped cosine to LPPL₁ (9 parameters). The primary remains a damped log-periodic; the secondary is a constant-amplitude modulation. Typically captures a faster ω₂≈20 beat on top of the ω≈9 primary. Whether ω₂ is a genuine cycle or a harmonic/artifact of the primary is model-dependent — watch its stability across windows.

Damped primary + undamped secondary. Track how W₂ evolves — flips between ~9 and ~21 depending on which regime dominates the window.

LPPL₃ (12 params, 7yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t) + C₁·t⁻ᴰ·cos(ω₁·ln t + φ₁) + C₂·cos(ω₂·ln t + φ₂) + C₃·cos(ω₃·ln t + φ₃)

LPPL₃ (3 frequencies) — three log-periodic cosines (12 parameters). With carefully separated frequencies this is the honest LPPL extension: enough degrees of freedom to represent Bitcoin's observed fast (ω≈7–10), medium (ω≈15–20), and slow (ω≈25–35) cycle components, but not so many that you start fitting noise. R² on full history is comparable to LPPL₄ with one fewer frequency.

Three frequencies in a 7-year window. Enough data for primary + secondary ω≈21 cycle identification; tight on ω≈9.

LinPPL (6 params, 5yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t) + C·t⁻ᴰ·cos(ω_cal·t + φ) [oscillation in calendar time, not log-time]

LinPPL (Linear-Periodic Power Law) — our variant: the oscillation lives in CALENDAR time (cos(ω_cal·t)) rather than log-time (cos(ω·ln t)). 6 parameters. Period T = 2π/ω_cal stays constant in years, designed to match Bitcoin's ~4-year halving cycle directly. Unlike LPPL₁, the cycle does not compress toward a singularity — it's a steady calendar metronome on top of the power-law baseline. Full-history R² is close to LPPL₁ but with very different interpretation.

Linear-periodic variant: oscillation in CALENDAR time (W_cal·t) rather than log-time. Period T=2π/W_cal stays constant in years — designed to match Bitcoin's ~4-year halving cycle directly. Expect T to track the halving cycle more stably than LPPL₁'s W.

HybPPL (9 params, 5yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t) + C₁·t⁻ᴰ·cos(ω_log·ln t + φ₁) + C₂·cos(ω_cal·t + φ₂) [log-periodic damped + linear-periodic undamped]

HybPPL (Hybrid log+linear PPL) — our best-fitting 9-parameter model: combines a damped log-periodic primary (cos(ω_log·ln t)) with an undamped linear-periodic secondary (cos(ω_cal·t)). Two cleanly separated mechanisms in one model: a log-time-compressing damped chirp (fading over time) plus a calendar metronome tracking halvings (persistent). Full-history R²≈0.989 — ties LPPL₃ (12 params) at 9 params, beats LPPL₂ at the same param count. The two oscillators live on different time axes (log-time vs calendar time), so unlike multi-frequency LPPL they do not drift into stable intermodulation beats against each other.

PL 6mo timing — unbounded OLS

PL timing analysis — aligns the rolling slope B(t) with log₁₀(price) at the same window end, then computes Pearson correlations (zero-lag) and lagged cross-correlation over ±24 months. B is a trailing window-width growth rate; its peaks lag price rallies until the bubble fully enters the window, and subsequent crashes drag B down until the crash fills the window. Key measurements to compare: peak |xcorr(B, log-excess)| and the lag at which it occurs. B vs 6-month forward momentum is the most direct timing read — strongly-negative means an elevated slope NOW predicts near-term drawdown.

Using PL 6mo / 1yr / 2yr to predict cycle bottoms

Signal mechanics. The N-year rolling PL slope BN(t) is the net log-log growth rate over the window [t−N, t]. It's a trailing indicator: BN peaks when a bull run has just fully entered the window, then declines as the window fills with the post-peak crash, and bottoms some months after the bubble top depending on the window width vs. the crash length.

Observed cross-correlation. corr(BN, log-excess) peaks at lag −L months, where L grows with window width but not linearly. Measured across three widths:

  • 6mo window: L = 12 months, peak |corr| = 0.49
  • 1yr window: L = 13 months, peak |corr| = 0.66
  • 2yr window: L = 22 months, peak |corr| = 0.80

The peak is negative at each width because B is low after bubble peaks and high after bear bottoms. For windows shorter than the typical 12-month crash duration the lag bottoms out near ~12 months (the crash fills the window before the window width does). For windows wider than the crash, the lag grows roughly with window width. Correlation strength grows monotonically with window width because the signal averages more noise out.

Bottom-prediction recipe.

  1. Anchor on the last bubble top. Identify the most recent log-excess peak date Tpeak from the /A palette-chart or the QR bubble overlay.
  2. Expected B6mo trough: Tpeak + ~12 months (fast alert — the half-year window is fully saturated with crash data by month 12).
  3. Expected B1yr trough: Tpeak + ~13 months. 12 months of crash have replaced 12 months of rally in the 1-year window.
  4. Expected B2yr trough: Tpeak + ~22 months. The wider window captures the full rally + crash + post-crash chop before bottoming.
  5. Zero-crossings as confirmation. Watch B1yr cross zero upward (window's net return turns positive) — typically arrives a few months before B1yr reaches its trough and often coincides with the actual price bottom. B2yr crossing zero is a stronger, later confirmation, usually already in early bull.
  6. Corroboration rule. When B1yr troughs AND starts rising, and B2yr is still falling but decelerating, you are in the late-bear / early-accumulation window. When B2yr troughs and B1yr is already above zero climbing, the cycle bottom is in the rear-view mirror.
  7. Asymmetry caveat. The recipe's average-lag formula is noisy per-cycle. Individual cycles can differ by several months, and the lag depends on the actual crash length — shorter crashes pull the trough earlier. Divergence between 1yr and 2yr trough timings is itself diagnostic of cycle-length shifts.

Anchoring on Tpeak. The algorithm uses the peak of log-excess (log-price minus long-run PL), not the nominal price peak. For the 2021 cycle the log-excess peak is 2021-03 ($58k rally top, where price led trend by the most), not the 2021-11 nominal top — because the long-run PL was rising fast enough by November that the relative-to-trend excess had already declined from its March high. The timing charts annotate predictions from the log-excess peaks automatically.

Backtest (2021 cycle, Tpeak = 2021-03 log-excess). B1yr predicted 2022-04, observed 2022-10 (+6mo from formula); B2yr predicted 2023-01, observed 2023-02 (+1mo from formula). The 2yr prediction is much more accurate per-cycle because its lag is set by the genuine 22mo window-fill physics, while the 1yr lag is capped at the crash-duration (~12mo) and depends more strongly on the actual crash shape.
