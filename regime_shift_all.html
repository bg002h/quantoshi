Power Law (2 params, 2yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t)

Power Law (PL) — the simplest Bitcoin price model. Pure log-log linear fit: price grows as a fixed power of time since genesis. Two parameters (intercept A, slope B) and no oscillation. Sliding-window fits reveal how the effective growth exponent evolves as different eras of Bitcoin history enter and leave the fit window. Here we fit by unbounded OLS (closed-form) so the true slope is never clipped; a centered intercept A' = mean log-price in the window is also plotted — A' is orthogonal to B, while raw A is a mechanical mirror of B (see FAQ on A/B anti-correlation).

Shortest power-law window — reacts fastest to regime changes. B_pl oscillates strongly around bubble peaks and bear bottoms (slope over-fits to local cycle phase). Use alongside wider windows to separate transient spikes from secular drift.

LPPL₁ (6 params, 5yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t) + C·t⁻ᴰ·cos(ω·ln t + φ)

LPPL₁ (Log-Periodic Power Law, 1 frequency) — Sornette's classic bubble model: power-law trend plus one log-periodic oscillation whose peaks compress in log-time toward a finite-time singularity. 6 parameters. The angular frequency ω encodes the bubble's discrete scale invariance; historically ω≈7 was the canonical value, but modern Bitcoin fits prefer ω≈15–20 (cycle-stretching). D is the critical exponent.

Single damped log-periodic oscillation. W hits upper bound (15) from ~2020 onward — a signature of Bitcoin's cycle stretching.

LPPL₂ (9 params, 5yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t) + C₁·t⁻ᴰ·cos(ω₁·ln t + φ₁) + C₂·cos(ω₂·ln t + φ₂)

LPPL₂ (2 frequencies) — adds a second, undamped cosine to LPPL₁ (9 parameters). The primary remains a damped log-periodic; the secondary is a constant-amplitude modulation. Typically captures a faster ω₂≈20 beat on top of the ω≈9 primary. Whether ω₂ is a genuine cycle or a harmonic/artifact of the primary is model-dependent — watch its stability across windows.

Damped primary + undamped secondary. Track how W₂ evolves — flips between ~9 and ~21 depending on which regime dominates the window.

LPPL₃ (12 params, 7yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t) + C₁·t⁻ᴰ·cos(ω₁·ln t + φ₁) + C₂·cos(ω₂·ln t + φ₂) + C₃·cos(ω₃·ln t + φ₃)

LPPL₃ (3 frequencies) — three log-periodic cosines (12 parameters). With carefully separated frequencies this is the honest LPPL extension: enough degrees of freedom to represent Bitcoin's observed fast (ω≈7–10), medium (ω≈15–20), and slow (ω≈25–35) cycle components, but not so many that you start fitting noise. R² on full history is comparable to LPPL₄ with one fewer frequency.

Three frequencies in a 7-year window. Enough data for primary + secondary ω≈21 cycle identification; tight on ω≈9.

LinPPL (6 params, 5yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t) + C·t⁻ᴰ·cos(ω_cal·t + φ) [oscillation in calendar time, not log-time]

LinPPL (Linear-Periodic Power Law) — our variant: the oscillation lives in CALENDAR time (cos(ω_cal·t)) rather than log-time (cos(ω·ln t)). 6 parameters. Period T = 2π/ω_cal stays constant in years, designed to match Bitcoin's ~4-year halving cycle directly. Unlike LPPL₁, the cycle does not compress toward a singularity — it's a steady calendar metronome on top of the power-law baseline. Full-history R² is close to LPPL₁ but with very different interpretation.

Linear-periodic variant: oscillation in CALENDAR time (W_cal·t) rather than log-time. Period T=2π/W_cal stays constant in years — designed to match Bitcoin's ~4-year halving cycle directly. Expect T to track the halving cycle more stably than LPPL₁'s W.

HybPPL (9 params, 5yr windows)

log₁₀(price) = A + B·log₁₀(t) + C₁·t⁻ᴰ·cos(ω_log·ln t + φ₁) + C₂·cos(ω_cal·t + φ₂) [log-periodic damped + linear-periodic undamped]

HybPPL (Hybrid log+linear PPL) — our best-fitting 9-parameter model: combines a damped log-periodic primary (cos(ω_log·ln t)) with an undamped linear-periodic secondary (cos(ω_cal·t)). Two cleanly separated mechanisms in one model: one log-time chirp converging toward a singularity and one calendar metronome tracking halvings. Full-history R²≈0.989 — ties LPPL₃ (12 params) at 9 params, beats LPPL₂ at the same param count. No intermod artifacts — the two frequencies live on different time axes so they can't alias each other.

Combines log-periodic damped primary + linear-periodic undamped secondary. Full-history R²=0.989 — beats LPPL₂ at same 9-param count. Watch how the relative contribution of W_log vs W_cal evolves over windows.

PL 2yr timing — unbounded OLS

PL 2yr timing analysis — aligns the 2yr rolling slope B(t) with log₁₀(price) at the same window end, then computes Pearson correlations (zero-lag) and lagged cross-correlation over ±24 months. The B trace is a trailing 2-year growth rate; its peaks lag price rallies until the bubble fully enters the window, and subsequent crashes drag B down for another ~2 years. Key measurements to compare: peak |xcorr(B, log-excess)| and the lag at which it occurs. B vs 6-month forward momentum is the most direct timing read — strongly-negative means a high 2yr slope predicts near-term drawdown.
